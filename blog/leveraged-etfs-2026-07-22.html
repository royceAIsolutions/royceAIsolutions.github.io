⚡ Volatility Decay Analysis — What 3x Costs You

Volatility decay remains the single biggest hidden cost of holding leveraged ETFs. Let's look at SOXL since its June 22 high to see it in action:

Between June 22 (high $302) and July 22 (close $163.91), the SOX semiconductor index fell roughly 30%. You'd expect SOXL to be down approximately 3x that — 90% — putting it around $30. But SOXL is at $163.91, down only 46%. Why? Because the daily reset means the fund doesn't compound losses linearly. In a bear market with volatile bounces (like the +3.38% day today), the 3x structure actually protects you on the downside relative to a 3x static position. Every green day resets the base higher, preventing the catastrophic compounding that would occur in a straight-line drawdown.

The flip side: in a perfectly choppy market where the index goes nowhere (+1%, -1%, +1%, -1%), a 3x ETF will steadily bleed value due to negative compounding. Let's model it:

  • Day 1: Index +1% | SOXL expected +3% | SOXL = $100 → $103
  • Day 2: Index -1% | SOXL expected -3% | SOXL = $103 → $99.91
  • Day 3: Index +1% | SOXL = $99.91 → $102.91
  • Day 4: Index -1% | SOXL = $102.91 → $99.82

After four days of a flat market, SOXL has lost 0.18% to decay. Over 20 days of sideways chop, that compounds to roughly -3.5% in pure decay — money you lose even though the underlying went nowhere. This is why 3x ETFs are trading instruments, not investments. The maximum efficient hold is 1-3 days; beyond that, decay eats your returns. The 2x funds (QLD, SSO, NVDL, AVGU) suffer roughly half the decay, which is why they're better suited for 3-7 day swing holds.
