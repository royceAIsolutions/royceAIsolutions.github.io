Best Korean Restaurants in Orange County (2026)

Updated July 22, 2026 • 10 min read

Orange County is home to one of the largest and most vibrant Korean communities in the United States, and the food scene reflects that in the best possible way. From smoky Korean BBQ houses in Buena Park to cozy tofu stew joints in Irvine and Garden Grove, OC's Korean restaurant landscape rivals anything you'll find in LA's Koreatown — often with shorter waits and more parking.

This guide covers the absolute best Korean restaurants across Orange County, organized by category. Whether you're craving marinated short ribs, silky soft tofu stew, or a bowl of rich pork soup that tastes like Seoul, these are the spots you need to know.

TL;DR — Top Pick: Baekjeong in Buena Park is the best Korean BBQ experience in Orange County. For tofu, hit BCD Tofu House in Irvine. For the most authentic soup experience, Jinsol Gukbap in Buena Park is worth the drive from anywhere in OC.

Where Is OC's Koreatown?

Unlike Los Angeles, Orange County doesn't have a single officially designated Koreatown. Instead, Korean restaurants and businesses are clustered in four main areas:

  • Buena Park: The heaviest concentration along Beach Boulevard and Orangethorpe Avenue. This is OC's de facto Koreatown with Baekjeong, Jinsol Gukbap, Mo Ran Gaak, and dozens of Korean cafes and bakeries. The Buena Park Korean business district has grown explosively over the last decade.
  • Garden Grove: The original Korean hub in OC. Westminster Avenue and Garden Grove Boulevard are lined with Korean BBQ spots, tofu houses, and Korean-Chinese restaurants. More old-school and affordable than Buena Park.
  • Irvine: A younger, trendier Korean food scene concentrated around Culver Plaza and the Diamond Jamboree shopping center. Lots of Korean fusion, modern takes on classics, and Korean-owned dessert shops.
  • Fullerton: A growing strip along Euclid Street with less-crowded Korean BBQ and tofu spots, popular with CSUF students.
Pro tip: Buena Park has become the new center of gravity for Korean food in OC. If you only have one day to eat Korean in Orange County, spend it in Buena Park — you'll find everything from high-end KBBQ to 24-hour soup joints within a 2-mile radius.

🏆 The Best Korean BBQ in OC

1. Baekjeong (Buena Park) — The Gold Standard

Baekjeong

★★★★½ 4.5 / 5 on Yelp
📍 8051 Beach Blvd, Buena Park
💰 $$ — $40–60 per person for full BBQ experience
🕐 Open daily 11 AM – 11 PM (expect 30–60 min wait on weekends)
Must-Order: Pork Belly, LA Kalbi, Beef Brisket
💡 Pro tip: Go on a weekday around 4 PM to avoid the dinner rush. Order the combo #1 (pork belly + marinated short rib) — it covers the essentials and comes with a free steamed egg.

Baekjeong (pronounced "BaeK-Jung") is the flagship Korean BBQ chain from celebrity chef Baek Jong-won, and the Buena Park location is the only one in Orange County. It's widely regarded as the best KBBQ in OC, and for good reason. The meats are premium quality — thick-cut pork belly that sizzles and crisps up on the gas grill, marinated LA galbi so tender it falls off the bone, and brisket that cooks in seconds on the domed grill top.

The banchan (side dishes) here are exceptional and refillable: soybean paste dip, fresh lettuce wrap greens, pickled radish, kimchi, bean sprouts, and a signature cheese corn side that's dangerously addictive. Every table gets a free steamed egg soufflé and a bowl of doenjang jjigae to finish. The staff handles the grilling for you, which means perfectly cooked meat every time.

What to order: Go for the combo menus. Combo #1 ($69.99 for two, prices as of mid-2026) gets you pork belly and marinated short rib. Add the beef brisket separately ($24.99). Order a bottle of soju (Chamisul or Chum Churum, around $18) to complete the experience. The kimchi jjigae at the end of the meal is a must — they cook it table-side with the leftover meat drippings.

2. Mo Ran Gaak (Buena Park) — Premium Aged KBBQ

Mo Ran Gaak

★★★★½ 4.4 / 5 on Yelp
📍 5255 Beach Blvd, Buena Park
💰 $$ — $45–70 per person
🕐 Open daily 11 AM – 10 PM
Must-Order: Aged Pork Belly, Beef Ribeye, Cold Noodles
💡 Pro tip: Mo Ran Gaak specializes in aged, dry-rubbed pork belly. Unlike the standard cuts at most KBBQ spots, their pork goes through a multi-day aging process that concentrates the flavor. It's worth the premium.

Mo Ran Gaak takes Korean BBQ up a notch with their signature aged meats. The pork belly undergoes a dry-aging process that gives it a deeper, almost nutty flavor and a tender texture you won't find at most BBQ houses. The space is more upscale than your typical KBBQ joint — think dark wood, mood lighting, and a more refined atmosphere. It's a great pick for a date night or when you want to impress out-of-town guests.

What to order: The aged samgyeopsal (pork belly) is the star here — don't skip it. The beef ribeye is also excellent, marbled beautifully and grilled to perfection. Finish with naengmyeon (cold buckwheat noodles) — the icy, tangy broth cuts through the richness of all that grilled meat. Expect to spend around $55–70 per person if you go with beef.

3. Gangnam Station (Irvine) — Best AYCE Value

Gangnam Station

★★★★ 4.0 / 5 on Yelp
📍 4400 Barranca Pkwy #140, Irvine (Culver Plaza)
💰 $ — AYCE lunch $22.99, dinner $29.99
🕐 Open daily 11 AM – 10 PM
Must-Order: Beef Galbi, Spicy Pork Bulgogi, Seafood Pancake
💡 Pro tip: Go for lunch on weekdays — same quality, $7 cheaper. The spicy pork bulgogi is the best-value protein on the AYCE menu. Avoid peak dinner hours (6:30–8 PM) when the wait can hit 45 minutes.

Gangnam Station is the reigning champion of all-you-can-eat Korean BBQ in Irvine. At $22.99 for weekday lunch AYCE (as of mid-2026), it's an incredible deal. The quality is solid for AYCE — not Baekjeong-level, but perfectly respectable. The banchan spread is generous with the usual suspects: kimchi, bean sprouts, pickled radish, japchae, and a surprisingly good corn cheese.

The restaurant is spacious with excellent ventilation — you won't walk out smelling like a grill (which is rare for KBBQ). It's hugely popular with UCI students and Irvine families, so come early or be prepared to wait.

What to order on AYCE: Start with the beef galbi and spicy pork bulgogi (the standouts). Get a round of brisket for quick-grill satisfaction. The seafood pancake is available as a side — order it crispy. Pace yourself: the grill time on thinner cuts is 30–60 seconds, and the banchan refills keep coming.

4. Mr. BBQ (Fullerton) — Hidden Gem

Mr. BBQ

★★★★¼ 4.2 / 5 on Yelp
📍 1140 N Euclid St, Fullerton
💰 $ — AYCE $21.99–$29.99 depending on tier
🕐 Open Tue–Sun 11 AM – 10 PM
Must-Order: Beef Brisket, Pork Jowl, Marinated Chicken
💡 Pro tip: The premium AYCE tier ($29.99) includes beef brisket and pork jowl — worth the $8 upcharge. They also have a killer spicy rice cake (tteokbokki) side dish that most KBBQ places charge extra for.

Mr. BBQ in Fullerton is a quieter alternative to the Buena Park and Irvine crowds. It's a solid AYCE option with three pricing tiers. The service is fast, the grills are changed frequently (a sign of a well-run KBBQ spot), and the banchan is fresh. It's particularly popular with CSUF students and north OC locals.

🍲 The Best Tofu Houses (Soondoobu)

5. BCD Tofu House (Irvine) — The Gold Standard for Tofu Stew

BCD Tofu House

★★★★¼ 4.2 / 5 on Yelp
📍 2700 Alton Pkwy #137, Irvine (Diamond Jamboree)
💰 $ — $14.99–18.99 per combo
🕐 Open daily 24 hours (yes, 24/7)
Must-Order: Soon Tofu Combo (medium spice), Galbi Combo, Kombu (Seaweed) Tofu
💡 Pro tip: BCD is open 24 hours — it's the go-to late-night spot after KBBQ or drinks. Order the "combo" which comes with a choice of galbi or bulgogi on the side. The stone pot bibimbap is included with every combo. Ask for a raw egg to crack into the bubbling stew.

BCD Tofu House is a Korean institution. Founded in Beverly Hills in 1996 and now with locations across the country, the Irvine outpost at Diamond Jamboree delivers exactly what you expect: bubbling-hot soondoobu jjigae served in a stone pot, delivered to your table still actively boiling. The server cracks a raw egg into the stew at your table — stir it in for extra richness.

The combo deal is where it's at: any tofu stew comes with a choice of galbi (two short ribs) or bulgogi, plus a stone pot bibimbap and all the banchan (the fish cake and the crispy seaweed are highlights). The galbi combo is the best value at around $18.99. The seafood tofu stew (haemul soondoobu) with clams, shrimp, and mussels is excellent. Spice levels go from mild (1) to extra hot (5) — most people find level 3 a comfortable kick.

Pro-level move: BCD has a secret menu item called "Kombu Tofu" — seaweed soft tofu stew. It's cleaner, more delicate, and intensely umami from the kelp broth. You have to specifically request it.

6. YD Tofu House (Garden Grove) — Old-School Favorite

YD Tofu House

★★★★ 4.1 / 5 on Yelp
📍 10052 Garden Grove Blvd, Garden Grove
💰 $ — $12.99–16.99 per combo
🕐 Open 7 AM – 10 PM daily
Must-Order: Pork & Kimchi Tofu, Original Soon Tofu, Galbi Combo
💡 Pro tip: YD serves breakfast! They open at 7 AM and serve soondoobu from the morning. It's an incredible OC breakfast move that nobody talks about. The pork and kimchi version is uniquely savory and not available at BCD.

YD Tofu House is the old-guard institution of Garden Grove's Korean food corridor. It's been around for decades and has a loyal local following. The vibe is no-frills, the service is efficient, and the tofu stew is consistently excellent. If BCD is the polished chain, YD is the neighborhood spot that's been perfecting the same recipes for 30+ years.

What to order: The pork and kimchi tofu stew is the standout — the kimchi is deeply fermented and adds a tangy, savory depth that the cleaner seafood versions don't have. The galbi combo here is slightly cheaper than BCD and the banchan includes a fantastic pickled cucumber salad.

🥣 The Best Korean Soups & Stews

7. Jinsol Gukbap (Buena Park) — Legendary Pork Soup

Jinsol Gukbap

★★★★ 4.0 / 5 on Yelp
📍 7446 Orangethorpe Ave, Buena Park
💰 $ — $10.99–14.99 per bowl
🕐 Open 7 AM – 11 PM daily
Must-Order: Dwaeji Gukbap, Bossam, Soondoobu Jjigae
💡 Pro tip: Add the ssamjang (spicy dipping sauce) and kimchi to your gukbap — it transforms the broth. The bossam (boiled pork wrap) pairs perfectly with the soup. The Irvine location closed — Buena Park and KTown LA are the only remaining spots.

Jinsol Gukbap is the real deal — a Seoul-style pork soup house that originally had an Irvine location (sadly, it closed). The Buena Park location on Orangethorpe is where the faithful go for dwaeji gukbap: a rich, milky pork bone broth simmered for hours until it's almost creamy, filled with tender sliced pork shoulder. You season it yourself with salt, pepper, and chopped scallions at the table.

The bossam (boiled pork belly wraps) here is excellent — tender pork with a perfect fat-to-meat ratio, served with fresh lettuce, ssamjang, and raw garlic. Wrap a slice of pork with kimchi and garlic, dip in ssamjang, and chase it with a spoonful of the soup. It's a transcendent combination.

What to order: Dwaeji gukbap ($12.99) is the signature. The soondoobu jjigae is also surprisingly good for a place that specializes in pork soup. If you're with 2+ people, add the bossam ($28.99 large) for sharing. The portions are generous — one bowl of gukbap is a full meal.

🥟 Banchan & Korean Sides Worth Seeking Out

One of the great pleasures of Korean dining is the banchan — those endless small side dishes that arrive before your meal. While most Korean BBQ and tofu house meals come with complimentary banchan, these spots are worth visiting just for their side dish game:

8. Hamjipark (Buena Park) — Banchan Heaven

Hamjipark

★★★★¼ 4.2 / 5 on Yelp
📍 7361 Beach Blvd, Buena Park
💰 $$ — $15–25 per person
🕐 Open 11 AM – 9 PM daily
Must-Order: Haemul Pajeon, Japchae, Kimchi Jjigae
💡 Pro tip: The banchan here includes a housemade lotus root side that's sweet, tangy, and addictive. They also serve a miniature fried fish side that most Korean restaurants don't bother with anymore. Take your time eating the banchan before the main courses arrive — it's part of the experience.

Hamjipark is famous for its banchan selection — you'll get 10–12 different small dishes before your meal even starts. The haemul pajeon (seafood scallion pancake) is arguably the best in OC: crispy on the outside, chewy on the inside, packed with shrimp and squid. The japchae (glass noodles) is perfectly seasoned with sesame oil and soy.

🍜 Korean-Chinese & Other Must-Try Categories

9. Yangji Gamjatang (Garden Grove) — Pork Bone Soup Specialists

Yangji Gamjatang

★★★★ 4.0 / 5 on Yelp
📍 9681 Garden Grove Blvd, Garden Grove
💰 $ — $15.99–22.99 per pot
🕐 Open 11 AM – 10 PM daily
Must-Order: Gamjatang (pork bone soup), Kimchi Jjigae
💡 Pro tip: Gamjatang is a legendary Korean hangover cure. The perilla seed powder in the broth gives it a uniquely nutty, earthy flavor. Use your hands to pull the meat off the bones — it's falling-apart tender. Two people can share one large pot easily.

Gamjatang (pork bone soup) is one of Korea's great comfort foods, and Yangji in Garden Grove does it better than anyone in OC. The broth is rich with pork flavor, slightly spicy from gochugaru (Korean red pepper flakes), and thickened with perilla seed powder. The pork backbone pieces are enormous, with tender meat that separates from the bone with the slightest tug. The potato chunks (gamja = potato in Korean) soak up all that savory broth.

10. Kaju Tofu House (Irvine) — Vegan-Friendly Korean

Kaju Tofu House

★★★★ 4.0 / 5 on Yelp
📍 5321 University Dr #112, Irvine
💰 $ — $11.99–15.99 per combo
🕐 Open 10:30 AM – 9:30 PM daily
Must-Order: Vegetable Soon Tofu, Mushroom Soon Tofu, Galbi Combo
💡 Pro tip: Kaju has several vegan/vegetarian tofu options that use mushroom broth instead of the traditional meat-based stock. The vegetable and mushroom soondoobu are genuinely delicious — not just token options. This is the best Korean spot in OC to bring vegetarian friends.

Tucked into the University Town Center by UC Irvine, Kaju Tofu House is a quieter, more affordable tofu option that's popular with students and vegetarians alike. They offer mushroom and vegetable broth options that are genuinely flavorful (most Korean spots just leave the meat out and call it "vegetarian" — Kaju actually builds flavor with shiitake and king oyster mushrooms).

🍶 Korean Cooking at Home: A Banchan Shopping Guide

If you want to recreate the Korean restaurant experience at home, these OC Korean markets have everything you need:

  • Zion Market (Irvine & Buena Park) — The best Korean grocery chain in OC. Their banchan bar has 20+ prepared side dishes. Pick up pre-marinated bulgogi and galbi from the meat counter — it's the same marinade most restaurants use.
  • H Mart (Irvine & Buena Park) — Great for Korean pantry staples: gochujang, doenjang, sesame oil, and gochugaru. The food court at the Irvine H Mart is worth a visit for the tteokbokki and kimbap.
  • Arirang Market (Garden Grove) — Smaller but has the best selection of Korean produce: napa cabbage, Korean radish, perilla leaves, and fresh ssam lettuce.
Pricing note: All prices listed are as of mid-2026 and are approximate. Orange County Korean restaurants have seen moderate price increases over the past year, but they remain considerably cheaper than comparable meals in LA. Expect to pay 10–15% less at most OC spots compared to Koreatown LA.

Final Tips for Korean Eating in OC

1. Bring cash or cards accepted. Most Korean restaurants in OC accept all major cards now, but a few old-school joints in Garden Grove are still cash-preferred. Check before you go.

2. The best banchan is served at the start. Don't fill up on it — but also don't ignore it. The pickled radish and ssamjang are meant to be paired with your grilled meat. Build the perfect bite: lettuce wrap + rice + meat + ssamjang + grilled kimchi + raw garlic.

3. Weekday lunch is the move. Every single KBBQ and tofu house in OC is cheaper at lunch. AYCE spots are $5–8 cheaper, and the meat quality is identical. Dinner prices at most spots also spike on Friday and Saturday nights.

4. Don't skip the soju. Korean food and soju are a perfect pairing. Most OC Korean restaurants carry Chamisul, Chum Churum, and flavored sojus (grapefruit, peach, yogurt). A bottle typically runs $15–20 at a restaurant. For the full experience, mix soju with beer for a "somaek" — pour soju first, then top with beer, give it a quick stir, and drink.

5. Plan for leftovers. Korean BBQ and stew portions are generous. Most spots will pack your leftovers, and the marinated meats actually taste even better the next day. Jinsol gukbap broth can be frozen and reheated beautifully.

Orange County's Korean food scene is world-class, and it keeps getting better. Whether you're making the pilgrimage to Baekjeong in Buena Park, grabbing a late-night tofu fix at BCD in Irvine, or chasing the perfect bowl of gamjatang in Garden Grove, you're eating some of the best Korean food available outside of Seoul.
