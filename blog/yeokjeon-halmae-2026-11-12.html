역전 할매: a 2,200-won beer empire that outgrew its own economics

역전할머니맥주 built ~960 pubs on the cheapest draft beer in Korea and a station-front location model. Between 2022 and 2025 its parent went from a 30% operating margin to 3.5%, paid out 250억 in dividends against 38억 of net income, and shrank its equity by a quarter. Here is the financial read — including a test of the claim that the business profits $50,000–$100,000 a month, and an appraisal of what the store is worth against 43 live Orange County comparables.

1. What the business actually is

역전 할매 ("the grandma in front of the station") is the shorthand customers use for 역전할머니맥주, operated by 역전에프앤씨. The proposition is a single number: draft beer at roughly 2,200 won a glass (about $1.65), served with cheap, salty anju in a no-frills room near a station or a university gate. Revenue in a franchise like this does not come from the beer margin at the table — it comes from selling supply to the franchisee (beer, food, fixtures, signage) plus fees, across as many stores as the brand can hold.

That distinction decides every number below. The company's revenue is roughly ₩108 million a year per store — the wholesale value of what each franchisee buys — not the store's own sales.

3. Where the profit went

The interesting part is that gross margin did not collapse. 뉴스워커 has gross margin at 42.7% → 43.4% across the two years — the supply business still earns its markup. What destroyed operating profit was fixed cost: promotions, labour, logistics, and a second growth axis (bringing the US sandwich brand Jimmy John's into Korea). Operating margin fell from ~30% (2021–22) to 9.1% (2024) to 3.5% (2025).

That pattern matters for how to read the business. A franchisor at a 30% margin is a royalty machine: each new store is close to pure profit. At 3.5%, the same store count barely covers the head office, and every new initiative has to be paid for out of a shrinking pool. Store growth stopped being self-funding somewhere between 2022 and 2024.

4. The cash that left the building

In June 2022, 케이스톤파트너스 (Keiston Partners) bought 100% of 역전에프앤씨 from founder 소종근 for about ₩145–150 billion, out of a ₩530 billion fourth blind fund. Within roughly the first year the company paid out around ₩60 billion in dividends — about 40% of the invested capital — leaving retained earnings near zero.

Then the business turned. FY2024 net income was ₩4.3 billion and the dividend was ₩5.0 billion. FY2025 was a ₩0.5 billion net loss and the dividend was ₩20 billion.

The two-year arithmetic: ₩3.8 billion of net income against ₩25 billion of dividends — 6.6× — while total equity fell from ₩86.1 billion to ₩65.6 billion (−₩20.5 billion). The company did not fund that distribution out of earnings; it funded it out of the balance sheet.

This is a dividend recapitalisation, not a scandal. It is the standard way a fund harvests a mature asset before a sale: take the cash out now, sell the remaining cash flow later. But it has a cost the buyer inherits — less equity, less reinvestment in brand and store quality, and a head office that has to defend a 2,200-won price point while its own cushion shrinks. The hygiene-inspection ranking (투다리 and 역전할머니맥주 as the worst two, 뉴스1, Sep 2025) and the portion-size dispute over the squid dish (위즈경제, Sep 2026) are the kind of finding that shows up when franchisees are squeezed at both ends.

5. What the exit is worth now

In 2022 the fund marked enterprise value above ₩220 billion on ₩34 billion of EBITDA. With FY2025 operating profit at ₩3.6 billion, EBITDA is plausibly in the ₩8–15 billion range once depreciation on company stores and lease assets is added back — an estimate, since the D&A line is not in the public series. Against a ₩145–150 billion entry price and ₩85 billion already taken out in dividends, that is roughly break-even at best on the equity — which explains the timing of everything else in this story. 뉴스핌 notes the holding is now 4 years 3 months old, past the usual exit window for a Korean PEF, and three of the four PEF-owned food companies it surveyed are loss-making. A seller needs a growth story at that point, not a cost story.

6. The growth story: Buena Park and Ho Chi Minh City

Two moves, both announced in 2026:

  • United States — first store in Buena Park, California (Orange County), open since June 2026, following a May announcement framing it as a college-district play (연합뉴스, May 15, 2026; 미주중앙일보, Jun 24, 2026 — that early coverage datelined the opening to neighbouring Fullerton). Buena Park is the right kind of catchment: a dense Korean-American corridor along Beach Blvd, a university base nearby, and a beer culture where a $7–9 draft is normal — which makes a $3–4 glass a genuine wedge rather than a novelty. The plan on the record is 50 US franchise locations, rolling out to LA, San Diego and Las Vegas after the Orange County opening (미주한국일보, Aug 12, 2026).
  • Vietnam — a ~300-pyeong (≈1,000 m²) flagship in Ho Chi Minh City, targeted for January 2027 (한국경제, 동아일보, Oct 30, 2026).

The US is the more interesting bet because the price gap is real and the reference price is high; Vietnam is the more familiar one (K-food, Korean pub format, a large expat and local drinking culture). Both, though, are being launched by a company whose domestic operating margin is 3.5% — the expansion is being financed by the same balance sheet that the dividends already thinned. That is the central tension in the story.

7. "The owner says it profits $50,000–$100,000 a month"

This claim cannot be judged until you say which profit, of which entity. The readings below give opposite answers, and the arithmetic separates them cleanly.

Reading A — the franchisor (역전에프앤씨) nets $50k–100k/month

$50,000–$100,000 a month is $600,000–$1.2 million a year, or ₩8.0–16.0 billion at ₩1,334/$.

Verdict (A): as a claim about the net profit of the operating company, it is too high for FY2025 by any margin — that year was a net loss — and it is roughly 1.9×–3.7× FY2024's actual ₩4.3 billion. The one version that survives is if "profit" means operating profit before the head-office drag: FY2025 operating profit was ₩3.6 billion ($2.7 million, ~$225k/month) and FY2024 was ₩9.9 billion (~$620k/month). On an operating basis the claim is not just plausible, it is conservative for 2024. So the number is real if the words are "operating profit, 2024" — and wrong if they are "net profit, now".

Reading B — one franchisee store nets $50k–100k/month

This is where the claim breaks. Work from the top down:

  • The whole company's revenue is ₩104.1 billion across ~963 stores — ₩108 million ($81,000) of annual head-office revenue per store.
  • That ₩108 million is the franchisee's purchases, not its sales. If supply and fees are 10–15% of a store's turnover, a typical store sells roughly ₩0.7–1.1 billion a year ($0.5–0.8 million).
  • A well-run 주점 keeps 10–15% of sales at the store level after rent, labour and royalties: ₩70–165 million a year, or $4,400–$10,300 a month.
Verdict (B): $50,000–$100,000 a month of profit in one store would require either ₩3–4 billion of annual sales at a 15% store margin, or a 35–50% margin on ~₩1 billion of sales. Neither exists in a format that sells beer at 2,200 won. The claim overstates single-store profitability by roughly 5–10×. A top-decile store in a prime station location might reach $15,000–$25,000 a month in a good year; that is the ceiling, not the base case.

Reading C — the US unit, in dollars

The US unit is where the claim becomes testable, because the check is three to four times the Korean one. Everything below is in dollars; the model is stated so you can substitute his actuals. Press coverage has datelined the opening to Fullerton and to neighbouring Buena Park on the same retail corridor; the arithmetic does not change with the city, only the rent line.

  • The target. $50,000 a month is $600,000 a year. $100,000 a month is $1.2 million.
  • Revenue implied by margin. At a 15% store margin the low end needs $4.0M of sales and the high end $8.0M. At a more typical franchised-pub margin of 12%, it is $5.0M and $10.0M.
  • Covers. $4.5M at a $38 average check is 324 covers a day — busy, but ordinary for a beer hall beside a university. $8.0M at the same check is 577 a day, every day, which is not.
  • Sales per square foot. On 5,000 sq ft, $4.5M is $900/sq ft (top decile for casual dining, where good units run $500–700) and $8.0M is $1,600/sq ft — a number essentially no full-service restaurant in California reaches.
  • Beer volume. At $4 a glass and a 40% beverage mix, $4.5M is ~1,080 glasses a day, about 8.7 kegs. Real for a busy bar. On $8.0M it is ~19 kegs a day, which is a distributorship, not a pub.
  • Rent load. 6,000 sq ft at $40/sq ft NNN is $20,000 a month before the first pour, in a market where kitchen labour is priced at 30% of sales.

What would change the verdict

  • Entity. If "the owner" means the franchisor's owner (the fund), Reading A applies. If it means a store owner, Reading B applies. Almost every disagreement about this business turns out to be that confusion.
  • Period. 2024 was a ₩9.9 billion operating-profit year; 2025 was ₩3.6 billion; 2026 has the Jimmy John's investment and a first US store in the cost base. A number quoted without a year is a number about 2024.
  • Cash vs accrual. The company has paid ₩25 billion in dividends across two years in which it earned ₩3.8 billion. "Profit" that is distributed as a recap is not evidence of a business generating that profit.
  • One store vs a chain. Korea's per-store economics do not scale to a US price point, so a US store's profit tells you nothing about a Korean one — and vice versa.

Three things fall out of that table.

  • The multiple is 2.3×, not 3.5× or 5×. It is the market's median, not its floor. On it, every $10,000 a month of verified cash flow is worth $277,000 — under the $240,000–$420,000 band a seller will argue from, and nowhere near the 4–6× implied by a franchisor's brand presentation.
  • Nothing in the county claims $50,000 a month. The highest cash flow any of the 43 listings discloses is $350,000 a year — $29,000 a month. The seller's low end would make this store the best-performing for-sale restaurant in Orange County; his high end is 3.4× that. This is the strongest evidence in the file against a $50,000–$100,000 net, and it does not depend on his P&L at all.
  • The closest comp is the one to argue from. A Korean gastropub in Irvine — fried chicken, K-pop room, late-night trade, absentee-run, 2,529 sf — discloses $1.63M of revenue and $24,000 a month of net, and asks $680,000, or 2.36× its cash flow. Same concept, same county, documented P&L. A Fullerton unit with five months of history does not price above it without proving more.

Read against that, the claim has a scale problem. $50,000 a month at a 12% store margin needs $5.0M of sales — 1.5× to 1.8× the average Orange County full-service restaurant, and more than three times the closest comparable. On that comparable's 2,529 sq ft footprint, $5.0M is $1,977 per square foot; even the low-end $4.0M is $1,582, against the $645 the real Korean pub across the county is actually running. A store can beat the average. A five-month-old store cannot beat it by 85% and then be appraised on the claim.

Appraised value: $400,000–$700,000. That is 2.31× of the $173,000–$303,000 of cash flow a buyer can reasonably credit a five-month-old unit with — the arithmetic of the $2.0M–$2.5M sales band, discounted for a store whose best month sits inside its own sample. The ceiling is $900,000, and only if twelve months of processor data prove $350,000+ of cash flow, which is the most any comparable in this market discloses. Above $1.0M the buyer is paying more than the best cash-flowing restaurant for sale in Orange County is asking — for a store that has not finished its first year.

Which is what the deal structure should say out loud: pay the verified number now and put the rest on proof — 25% seller note, twelve months of payments against a documented baseline, 10–15% escrow, lease assignment and franchisor consent executed before any deposit. A seller who believes $50,000 a month takes that trade. One who only believes the asking price does not, and that is the cheapest lie detector in the file.

9. What to watch

  • Per-store revenue. ₩108M (FY2024) → ₩108M (FY2025) of head-office revenue per store, flat while store count grew. Flat per-store revenue plus falling margin is the signature of a saturated format.
  • The FY2026 filings. Does operating margin stabilise above 5%, or does Jimmy John's Korea plus the Buena Park build consume the year?
  • Store churn. The number that matters is net closures, not openings. A 963-store base losing franchisees is a different business from one adding them.
  • Sale process. A holding at 4+ years with a 3.5% margin is a seller. Any announced deal will be priced off EBITDA, and the price will be the verdict on the growth story.
Bottom line. 역전 할매 is a genuinely large, genuinely cheap brand — 963 stores, ₩104 billion of revenue, 2,200 won beer — whose profit engine has been dismantled by fixed costs and by ₩250억 of distributions against ₩38억 of earnings. The $50,000–$100,000-a-month figure is achievable as franchisor operating profit in a good year like 2024, and is not achievable as a single store's net profit — it overstates that by roughly 5–10×. Quote it with the entity and the year, or it is not a fact about this business. What the store is worth, priced off 43 live Orange County comparables: $400,000–$700,000, struck at the market's median 2.31× cash flow, with $900,000 the ceiling if twelve months of processor data prove it. The claim, taken at face value, would price the same store at $1.4M–$2.8M — 3.5 to 4 times what the county's own listings say a unit like this is worth.
