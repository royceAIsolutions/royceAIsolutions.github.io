Defender is Land Rover's off-road heart, reborn for the modern era. The ladder-on-unibody architecture, Terrain Response 2, and the sheer visual presence make it the go-anywhere vehicle with real character — from the 2-door 90 to the three-row 130. With engines from the 296-hp P300 to the 525-hp supercharged V8, and the new OCTA at the top of the range, Defender covers everything from family hauling to Dakar-style desert running.

What you need to know

  • Defender 90 is the 2-door short-wheelbase; the 110 is the 4-door medium wheelbase; the 130 is the 4-door long wheelbase with three rows.
  • Defender 90/110 engine line: P300 2.0L turbo I4 (296 hp / 295 lb-ft), P400 3.0L turbo I6 mild hybrid (395 hp / 406 lb-ft), P500 5.0L supercharged V8 (518 hp), P525 5.0L supercharged V8 (525 hp, top spec).
  • Defender 130 is different under the skin: its P300 is a detuned 3.0L I6 (296 hp / 350 lb-ft), NOT the 2.0L I4 the 90/110 use. Same badge, different engine.
  • The 130 offers P400 as standard and has no V8 or diesel in the US market.
  • Towing across the line: 8,200 lbs. P400 0-60: ~5.8s on the 110, ~6.6s on the 130. Real-world MPG on the 130: 15-18 combined.
  • The OCTA is the hardcore halo — Defender's most extreme model, built for desert running and rock crawling straight from the factory.

The engineering view

The OCTA is the hardcore halo — Defender's most extreme model, built for desert running and rock crawling straight from the factory.

For fans, the detail is the point — Land Rover builds vehicles where the specification sheet tells a real story about capability, luxury, and intent. Every generation refines the formula rather than replacing it, which is exactly why the brand's enthusiasts are among the most loyal in the industry.

Which Defender is for you

The three body styles answer three different questions. The 90 is the enthusiast's choice — two doors, short wheelbase, the most authentic Defender proportions and the best approach/departure angles. It's a weekend toy that happens to be a Land Rover. The 110 is the sweet spot for most owners: four doors, room for five plus gear, and the same capability in a more usable package. The 130 stretches everything — three rows, room for seven, and an 8,200-pound tow rating that makes it a legitimate family expedition vehicle. On the engine front, the P400's 395 hp is the one most buyers land on: plenty of punch, mild-hybrid efficiency, and the tow capability everyone needs. The V8s are the indulgence — 518 to 525 hp of supercharged theater that only the 90 and 110 can get.

The OCTA halo

Defender OCTA is the answer to the question nobody asked: what if Defender went full motorsport? It's the most extreme Defender ever — wider track, serious suspension upgrades, and the kind of capability that makes it at home in the desert at speed. It's not about volume; it's about proving what the platform can do and pushing the brand's credibility with enthusiasts who want the ultimate expression. Even if you never take one off the pavement, the OCTA's existence lifts the whole Defender line.

Why Defender matters now

Defender has become the cultural symbol of modern Land Rover. It's the vehicle that proved the brand could do rugged and premium at the same time, and it's attracted a whole new generation of owners who never considered an SUV before. The wait lists, the resale values, the social media presence — it all speaks to a vehicle that people form genuine attachments to. For fans, Defender is the keeper: the model that keeps Land Rover's off-road soul alive in the electric era.
