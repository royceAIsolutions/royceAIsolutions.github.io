JLR's electrification is happening in two waves: the Range Rover Electric and GT on new dedicated platforms, and Jaguar's full reinvention as an all-electric ultra-luxury brand. The EMA platform underpins the smaller Land Rovers and the new GT, while MLA carries the full-size Range Rover Electric. Hybrids keep the V8 character available for the customers who want it — a genuinely two-track strategy.

What you need to know

  • Range Rover GT is built on EMA — the Electric Modular Architecture — JLR's dedicated EV platform for smaller Land Rover models and future Jaguars.
  • The Range Rover Electric is the flagship EV: the full-size Range Rover with a fully electric powertrain, still on the flexible MLA platform.
  • Type 00 uses a bespoke Jaguar EV architecture with over 1,000 hp expected.
  • JLR's roadmap: every Jaguar will be electric, Land Rover gets electric flagships alongside its hybrid and V8 options.
  • PHEVs across the Range Rover line offer meaningful electric-only range for city driving while keeping V8-level capability for the open road.

The engineering view

For fans, the detail is the point — Land Rover builds vehicles where the specification sheet tells a real story about capability, luxury, and intent. Every generation refines the formula rather than replacing it, which is exactly why the brand's enthusiasts are among the most loyal in the industry.

The platforms behind the plan

Two architectures carry JLR's electric future. MLA (Modular Longitudinal Architecture) is the flexible one — it underpins today's full-size Range Rover and Range Rover Sport in combustion, hybrid, and plug-in forms, and it will also carry the full-size Range Rover Electric. EMA (Electric Modular Architecture) is the dedicated EV platform: born electric, no compromises for combustion, and the base for the Range Rover GT and the next-generation smaller Land Rovers. The two-platform approach lets JLR electrify its most important model first on proven hardware while the dedicated EV architecture matures for everything after.

The charging and ownership reality

For owners, the electric transition is about real-world usability. The plug-in hybrids already on sale deliver meaningful electric-only range for the daily commute — charge at home, drive electric all week, and keep the internal combustion engine for the weekend road trip. The full EVs coming next push further: the Range Rover Electric and the GT will need the fast-charging network to be genuinely useful as grand tourers, and JLR is positioning both as long-distance vehicles, not just city cars. The charging experience — reliability, speed, and convenience — will be the real test of the electric Range Rover promise.

Why hybrid matters to fans

The PHEV route is quietly the most important part of JLR's strategy for existing owners. It keeps the driving character fans love — the V8 soundtrack, the effortless torque, the long-range confidence — while cutting the running costs and emissions that made big SUVs a target. For the enthusiast, a plug-in hybrid Range Rover is the least-compromise answer: electric silence for the commute, V8 character for the road trip, and full off-road capability everywhere in between. The pure EVs get the headlines; the hybrids sell the cars.
