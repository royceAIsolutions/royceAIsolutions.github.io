Range Rover Today — August 19, 2026

Range Rover continues to define the luxury SUV segment it invented. Here's what makes the current generation — and what's coming next — worth knowing.

The current state of play

Range Rover is the benchmark for luxury SUVs — the car that invented the category in 1970 and still defines it. The current L460 generation pairs a serene, minimalist cabin with genuine off-road capability and a 5600-series of choices from plug-in hybrid to the supercharged V8. The brand is now going electric in a serious way: the fully electric Range Rover and the upcoming Range Rover GT grand tourer on the EMA platform mark the biggest powertrain shift in the model's history.

What you need to know

  • The Range Rover (L460) offers two refrigerator options: a rear console fridge with crystal glassware available only with the 4-seat Executive Class Comfort Plus package on LWB models (Autobiography LWB or SV Signature Suite on SV), and a standalone front armrest/center console fridge available on 5-seat SWB models that holds four 16 fl oz bottles at 41°F.
  • Heated armrests are tied to Executive Class Comfort rear seats — they are not a standalone option, the opposite of the front fridge.
  • Range Rover GT, the fifth member of the family, is an all-electric grand tourer on the new EMA (Electric Modular Architecture) platform with a coupe-proportioned 4-door silhouette. Full reveal expected late 2026, estimated $120K-$160K.
  • The GT's cabin is designed for calm — warm, reductive space, hidden technologies, class-leading second-row legroom, and Range Rover capability retained.
  • Key fob battery is a standard CR2032 coin cell — a five-minute DIY swap.

For fans, the detail is the point — Land Rover builds vehicles where the specification sheet tells a real story about capability, luxury, and intent. Every generation refines the formula rather than replacing it, which is exactly why the brand's enthusiasts are among the most loyal in the industry.

The model line, explained

The Range Rover family is now five vehicles with distinct personalities. The full-size Range Rover is the flagship, offered in standard and long wheelbase with four, five, or seven seats and engines from the plug-in hybrid to the 615-hp SV. The Range Rover Sport is the athletic one — lighter on its feet, sharper on the road, still deeply luxurious. The Velar is the design statement, the most minimalist interpretation of Range Rover's reductive design language. The Evoque is the city-sized entry point, premium but compact enough for urban living. And coming late 2026 is the Range Rover GT: a grand tourer that puts the Range Rover presence into a lower, sleeker, car-shaped silhouette. Every one of them carries the brand's defining traits — commanding seating position, serene ride, and the off-road capability that no rival can match.

Why Range Rover owners are different

Range Rover has one of the highest customer loyalty rates in the luxury segment, and it's not just the badge. Owners describe the feeling as sitting above the world — the high command driving position, the hush of the cabin, the quiet confidence of never being out of your depth on any road. There's also the practicality of it: a Range Rover does the school run, the airport run, and the mountain cabin run in the same day without breaking character. That versatility is the brand's real secret, and it's why the car holds value so well.

What's coming next

The electrification of Range Rover is the biggest story in the brand's 50-plus year history. The fully electric Range Rover on the MLA platform is the flagship EV, and the GT on the EMA platform pushes the design language somewhere genuinely new. For fans, the next 24 months are the most exciting period since the original launched — two new body styles, two new platforms, and the definitive proof that luxury and electric can coexist without losing any of the Range Rover character. Keep an eye on the late-2026 GT reveal; it's the one that will redefine what a Range Rover can be.
