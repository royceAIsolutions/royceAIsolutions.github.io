Five customers a week
walk out nameless

They pull in. We greet them. They take the brochure and drive off — and nothing goes in the book. Forty walk-ins a week at 12.4% unlogged is five names we cannot call: a delivery every two weeks, twenty-four a year.

The conversation was never written down — so the call that decides the sale never happens. Ten minutes, three requirements: log every walk-in, four steps on the floor, follow-up that runs until they buy. Then we run the drill.

Requirement 01 — log every opportunity

What an unlogged walk-in costs

Not close rate. The gap between the people who walked in and the names in the CRM.

40Guests walk in per week — this store's traffic count
12.4%Of qualified leads are never logged in the CRM (published benchmark)
5Lost names per week (40 × 12.4%)
10%Close rate on a lead somebody actually works
1 / 2 wksDeliveries nobody could call (5 × 10%)
24 / yrThe same loss every year

The chain, in one line: 40 walk-ins a week, 12.4% of them never written down — 5 names the store cannot call — and at a 10% close rate that is a delivery every two weeks, about 24 a year, from the same habit. A lead never written down cannot be called, counted or coached, and every other store number is measured against a denominator that is short by whatever walked out unrecorded.

SOURCES — 12.4%: Better Car People, 2023 Dealer Benchmark Report — qualified leads never logged in the dealer CRM. No published benchmark exists for walk-ins specifically, and a walk-in leaves less trail than a lead, so treat 12.4% as the floor, not the ceiling. The 40 walk-ins and the 10% close rate are this store’s own numbers to replace.

Step 1

The greeting

Thirty seconds, one question, and their name said back to them.

17.3%More spent per person with the name used
2×Upsells accepted, 48.4% vs 22.6%
15.9%Higher satisfaction unless overused
12%More satisfied where badges were worn
⏱ 30 secOf eye contact, then hello and your name
🏷 1 badgeWorn — your name read before it is asked
🗣 1 nameTheirs, once or twice — recognition, not a script
❓ 1 questionWhat they came in to look at
🗣 SAY IT LIKE THIS

At hello: “Welcome in — I’m Royce. What’s your name?”

Then use it, once: “Good to meet you, Jim — what did you come in to look at today?”

Twice is the whole technique — at hello, and when you hand them to the desk.

A greeting inside 30 seconds of eye contact — hello and your name, not a pitch. The badge does the reintroducing: they read your name off your chest, so you say it once. Theirs, once or twice, reads as recognition; in every sentence it reads as a script.

SOURCES — Journal of Retailing (2026), Call me by my name: spend +17.3%, upsell 22.6% → 48.4%, satisfaction +15.9%. Badges: Shopper Anonymous, 116,000 mystery shops in the UK, Australia and New Zealand — satisfaction +12%. Misnaming: Rank-Christman et al., J. Consumer Psychology (2017).

Steps 2, 3 and 4

The visit

Seat them, three questions, one vehicle presented well — and a next contact agreed before they drive off.

🪑 02 · Seat them, then discover
  • Ask permission: “May I sit with you and ask a few questions so we pick the right vehicle?” A guest in a chair talks
  • Who is it for — them, their kid, their property?
  • What do you drive now, and what is wrong with it?
  • What has to be true on delivery day — payment, trade, transport, first trip?
  • Done when you can repeat their answer back word for word.
🚙 03 · Present and test drive
  • One unit, chosen from those answers — not three
  • Feature, benefit, proof on the unit, standing at it with them
  • Before the keys, quickly: log them — “I’ll send you the link to this one — what’s the best number?” then your contact card by text. Name and number, both, into eLeads
  • Same moment: scan their driver’s licence and touch the desk — the drive is on the board before it leaves the lot
  • Then the drive: they drive, you listen for what changes. No drive, no pricing
💵 04 · The numbers — price, present, close
  • The unit is where this starts, not ends — the work from here is the profile, the numbers and the close
  • Before pricing, sit them down and build the full profile in the CRM — name, mobile, ZIP and address, trade, money down, who it is for
  • The desk prices from that profile, you present it — price, allowance, payment, term, rate, fees, priced to their ZIP
  • Go for the close — ask for the paperwork, and get the manager involved: the desk closes what a consultant cannot
  • Cannot close? Agree the day to follow up — a date, not “soon”

📱 THE NUMBER IS ASKED HERE, AT THE VEHICLE — not at hello. Your contact card goes by text: that is your direct line. Pricing comes from the eLeads CRM on the office number, so the quote is traceable. Ask their ZIP the same time — tax and fees follow where the vehicle is registered.

Requirement 03 — follow-up best practices

The follow-up clocks

Five touches in fourteen days, then thirty days to decide: engaged and still looking goes to nurture, silence ends the file. One committed contact beats ten remembered intentions.

80%Of sales need five or more follow-up contacts to close
48%Of salespeople never send a second one
89 daysAverage buying cycle, first look to keys (Cox Automotive)
📲 Same day

Consultant · in person, then by text

“It’s Royce from earlier — thank you for coming in. Here is the link to the vehicle you looked at.”

WHYThe link is why you had a number to ask for. Same day proves you heard which unit they stood at.

☎️ 24 hours

Consultant · one call or text

“Morning — Royce from yesterday. Did the link come through? If the payment or the delivery date is still open, tell me which one and I’ll answer it straight.”

WHYOne question they answer in seconds — whichever half they name is the real obstacle.

🚙 72 hours

Consultant · call, carrying something

“I found a second option that fits what you told me — want me to send the link?”

WHYA different vehicle answers a different question: it was the car, or it is the money.

📅 14 → 30 days

Consultant · manager reviews

“Before we close the file — is there a payment that would have made this a yes?”

WHYTwo weeks is where most stores quit. The salesperson keeps the file to thirty; the manager reviews it there to close the lead or keep it alive.

🔄 Nurture

Consultant · until they buy

“Still looking? Two used units came in that fit what you described, and your trade is worth more than it was in March.”

WHYThe average cycle runs 89 days, three times the window most stores work. Nurture runs until they buy or tell you no — it is not a way to keep dead files open.

📊 QUALITY OF ENGAGEMENT AFTER THEY LEFT Read before the next touch: link opened · replied with a question of their own · called back · appointment on the calendar. Sent is not engaged. Two silent touches mean change the channel or the offer — never the volume.

SOURCES — 80% / 48%: ZoomInfo, sales follow-up statistics (2026). 89 days: Cox Automotive, Car Buyer Journey — average buying cycle, as reported 2026. Customer Traac, The 90-Day Buyer (2026) — many buyers take 30+ days, most 60 to 90. Proactive Training Solutions (2026) — 30 days of 6–8 contacts, then monthly nurture.

Do, then drill

The drill

📱 PHONES OUT · TURN TO A NEIGHBOR

1) Get their number, the way you get it at the vehicle: “I’ll send you the link to this one — what’s the best number for it?”

2) Text them your contact card from your phone — no paper: “That’s me saved for a direct call. Anything on the numbers will come from our eLeads CRM on the office number.”

3) The call next morning: “It’s Royce from yesterday, on our office line — did the link come through?”

Real number, real text, once each — then swap.

Standing up, Friday meeting, every person once — ten minutes for the room. The words are the easy part; the flinch is what gets drilled out. Practice the basics and the customer experience is seamless and elevated — that is the whole argument for the drill. Thank you — questions?
