Every store loses
the same customer

I sell at Jaguar Land Rover Newport Beach. The pattern I see most Fridays: a guest walks the lot, gets greeted, takes a brochure, drives off — and the name never reaches the CRM. Nothing went wrong in the conversation. The loss is that the conversation was never written down, so the follow-up call that decides the sale never happens.

That is the ten minutes this session fixes: how we greet and log a walk-in, and what happens after they leave.

Requirement 03 — follow-up best practices

The follow-up clocks

One committed contact beats ten remembered intentions.

Same day

Note in the CRM and a call while the visit is still fresh.

24 hours

A personal call or text about their car — never a template.

72 hours

A second touch with something useful: a vehicle, a payment, an answer.

14 days

Five touches, then a decision from the desk instead of silence.

Then the desk decides: re-engage, reassign, or close the file. Silence is not a follow-up plan — it is the default that loses the deal.

Do, then drill

The 30-second drill

Nobody is asked to be a hero. Everybody says the words out loud, once, before they walk the floor.

TURN TO A PARTNER

Greet them, get their name and mobile number out loud, and write it down in front of them — in 30 seconds. Then swap.

Every person leaves having done it once. That is what changes the Monday.

The ask

One ask. One number.

What the GM can walk the floor and see by the end of the first week.

THE ASK

Every walk-in is logged before the guest reaches the door.

THE NUMBER

Door count against logged opportunities — walked daily, reviewed Friday.

That is the whole system: four steps on the floor, four follow-up clocks, and one number nobody has to interpret. Thank you — questions?
