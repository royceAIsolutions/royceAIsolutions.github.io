Five customers a week
walk out nameless

They pull in. We greet them. They take the brochure and drive off — and nothing goes in the book. Forty walk-ins a week at 12.4% unlogged is five names we cannot call: a delivery every two weeks, twenty-four a year.

The conversation was never written down — so the call that decides the sale never happens. Ten minutes, three requirements: log every walk-in, four steps on the floor, follow-up that runs until they buy. Then we run the drill.

Royce KohSales trainer candidate · Irvine, CA
Requirement 01 — log every opportunity

What an unlogged walk-in costs

Not close rate. The gap between the people who walked in and the names in the CRM.

40Guests walk in per week — this store's traffic count
12.4%Of qualified leads are never logged in the CRM (published benchmark)
5Lost names per week (40 × 12.4%)
10%Close rate on a lead somebody actually works
1 / 2 wksDeliveries nobody could call (5 × 10%)
24 / yrThe same loss every year

The chain, in one line: 40 walk-ins a week, 12.4% of them never written down — 5 names the store cannot call — and at a 10% close rate that is a delivery every two weeks, about 24 a year, from the same habit. A lead never written down cannot be called, counted or coached, and every other store number is measured against a denominator that is short by whatever walked out unrecorded.

SOURCES — 12.4%: Better Car People, 2023 Dealer Benchmark Report — qualified leads never logged in the dealer CRM. No published benchmark exists for walk-ins specifically, and a walk-in leaves less trail than a lead, so treat 12.4% as the floor, not the ceiling. The 40 walk-ins and the 10% close rate are this store’s own numbers to replace.

Requirement 02 — key steps in the store

Four steps, in this order

One process. Taught the same way at every store, audited the same way by the desk.

01

Greet and log

A greeting inside 30 seconds of eye contact — hello and your name, not a pitch. They can wave you off and look. Name and mobile get written when the conversation opens. Done when the name is in the log.

02

Discover

Three questions: who it is for, what they drive now, what has to be true on delivery day. Done when you can repeat their answer in their words.

03

Present one vehicle

One unit that fits those answers, not three. Feature, benefit, proof, standing at the vehicle. Done when they have seen that exact unit.

04

The desk prices it, you present it

You have landed them on one unit — that is the handoff. The desk turns it into the customer’s pricing: price, trade allowance, payment, term, rate, taxes and fees. You put it in front of them while they are still here, then agree a day, a time and a name for the next call. Done when the pricing is in writing and the next contact is set.

Step 1

Greet and log

The moment it is written, the store owns the follow-up clock — not the consultant's memory.

30 secOf eye contact, not of them being on the lot — the clock starts when they look at you, and stops at hello
2 thingsTheir name and their mobile number — the name at hello, the number at the vehicle, when there is something to send them
1 placeThe log, written where they can see the pen move — then into the CRM before the next up
SAY IT LIKE THIS

At hello: “Welcome in — I’m Royce. What’s your name?”

At the vehicle, phone already out: “I’ll send you the link to this one — what’s the best number for it?”

The clock is eye contact to hello — nothing else. Not the gate, not the parking spot, not the moment the door shuts. A guest looks up, you have thirty seconds for a greeting they can wave off. The name is asked once the conversation has opened, not while they are still taking their jacket off; the number is asked at the vehicle, when you have a link to send them. What gets written: name, mobile, what they drove in, and the one thing they said that matters. If they will not give a number, take the name and the vehicle and ask again at the desk — a name with no number can still be worked, a guest nobody wrote down cannot.

WHY THE MIDDLE: the published road to the sale has greet (step 5), discovery (6), walkaround (7), then the desk writes the worksheet (10). Contact details belong to discovery — after hello, before the drive — not to the greeting, which is only a greeting, and not to the end, where a guest can leave without ever giving it. Source — The Ad Department, The car sales process (road to the sale, 13 steps), read 2026.

Steps 2, 3 and 4

The visit

Three questions, one vehicle presented well, and a next contact agreed before they drive off — walking them out is the natural moment to settle it.

Discover
  • Who is it for — them, their kid, their property?
  • What do you drive now, and what is wrong with it?
  • What has to be true on delivery day — payment, trade, transport, first trip?
  • Done when you can repeat their answer back word for word.
Present
  • One unit, chosen from those answers — not three
  • Feature: what it has — engine, drivetrain, seats, towing
  • Benefit: where they can drive it, what it hauls, who fits on it
  • Proof: the spec sheet, the vehicle in front of them, the number in writing
The numbers
  • Your job ends at the unit: they have landed on this one
  • You hand the desk the facts — the vehicle, their trade, money down, name and number already logged
  • The desk produces the pricing for the customer: price, trade allowance, payment, term, rate, taxes and fees — tax and registration follow the store’s ZIP
  • You put the number where they can read it, then stop talking — and the next contact is agreed out loud

PRICED FOR THIS STORE — tax, registration and doc fees follow the ZIP code on the deal. Give us the store’s ZIP code, ideally the full street address, and every figure in this proposal is priced for this store instead of a national average.

Requirement 03 — follow-up best practices

The follow-up clocks

Five touches in fourteen days, then thirty days to decide: engaged and still looking goes to nurture, silence ends the file. One committed contact beats ten remembered intentions.

80%Of sales need five or more follow-up contacts to close
48%Of salespeople never send a second one
89 daysAverage buying cycle, first look to keys (Cox Automotive)
Same day

Consultant · in person, then by text

WORD TRACK“It’s Royce from earlier — thank you for coming in. Here is the link to the vehicle you looked at.”

WHYThe link is why you had a number to ask for. Same day proves you heard which unit they stood at.

24 hours

Consultant · one call or text

WORD TRACK“Morning — Royce from yesterday. Did the link come through? If the payment or the delivery date is still open, tell me which one and I’ll answer it straight.”

WHYOne question they answer in seconds — whichever half they name is the real obstacle.

72 hours

Consultant · call, carrying something

WORD TRACK“I found a second option that fits what you told me — want me to send the link?”

WHYA different vehicle answers a different question: it was the car, or it is the money.

14 → 30 days

Manager · close or carry

WORD TRACK“Before we close the file — is there a payment that would have made this a yes?”

WHYTwo weeks is where most stores quit. The file runs to thirty, then splits: no engagement means it closes in writing; still looking means nurture.

Nurture

Desk · until they buy

WORD TRACK“Still looking? Two used units came in that fit what you described, and your trade is worth more than it was in March.”

WHYThe average cycle runs 89 days, three times the window most stores work. Nurture runs until they buy or tell you no — it is not a way to keep dead files open.

QUALITY OF ENGAGEMENT AFTER THEY LEFT Read before the next touch: link opened · replied with a question of their own · called back · appointment on the calendar. Sent is not engaged. Two silent touches mean change the channel or the offer — never the volume.

SOURCES — 80% / 48%: ZoomInfo, sales follow-up statistics (2026). 89 days: Cox Automotive, Car Buyer Journey — average buying cycle, as reported 2026. Customer Traac, The 90-Day Buyer (2026) — many buyers take 30+ days, most 60 to 90. Proactive Training Solutions (2026) — 30 days of 6–8 contacts, then monthly nurture.

Do, then drill

The drill

Nobody on this floor needs teaching how to say hello. Two sentences carry the whole system, and they are the two most people flinch at.

TURN TO A PARTNER

Say each sentence out loud, once, no preamble — then swap.

1) The number, asked while the pen is already moving: “I’ll send you the link to this one — what’s the best number for it?”

2) The call next morning: “It’s Royce from yesterday — did the link come through?”

Both sentences, no warming up, then swap.

Standing up, Friday meeting, every person once — ten minutes for the room. The words are the easy part; the flinch is what gets drilled out. Thank you — questions?
