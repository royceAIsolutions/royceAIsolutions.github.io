Requirement 01

The importance of logging every sales opportunity

An unlogged walk-in is invisible in the report and impossible to follow up. The session makes logging the first measurable win — before close rate — and shows the store what the gap between the door and the CRM costs in dollars.

Any key steps in between entering and leaving the store

Four steps, in order, taught the same way at every store: greet and log, discover and write the right vehicle, present one vehicle well, then present the number and commit the next next contact agreed before they drive off — walking them out is the natural moment to settle it.

The 10-minute training

Minute by minute, exactly as the room receives it. Every segment has a goal, the words the trainer says, and a proof the store can measure that week.

00:00 — The hook and the stake (:60) One story: a Saturday walk-in who left with a name on paper, and one who left with nothing. Ask the room what the second one cost us. Land the stake — thirty walk-ins a week, one in four never written down, and the store cannot manage what it never recorded.
01:00 — Step 1, greet and log (:90) Greet inside 30 seconds, get the name, get the mobile number, and write it in front of the guest. The lesson: the moment a walk-in is a written lead, the store owns the follow-up clock — not the consultant's memory.
02:30 — Step 2, discover and write the right vehicle (:120) Three questions — who is it for, what are you driving now, what has to be true on delivery day. Log the vehicle the guest actually wants, not the one nearest the door, so the follow-up talks about the right car.
04:30 — Step 3, the walkaround and the presentation (:120) Feature to benefit to proof. Present one vehicle well instead of three badly, then write in the note exactly what the guest responded to. That note is what the next call starts from.
06:30 — Step 4, the desk and the next contact (:90) Land them on one unit, hand the desk the facts, and the desk produces the customer’s pricing — price, trade allowance, payment, term, rate, taxes and fees. You present it, then commit the next contact before they drive off: day, time and who calls. A walk-in who leaves with a scheduled callback is no longer a lost lead.
08:00 — Follow-up, the 24-hour and 72-hour clocks (:90) Same-day note and same-day call. Twenty-four hours: a personal call or text about their car, not a template. Seventy-two hours: a second touch with something useful — a vehicle, a payment, an answer. Five touches over fourteen days, logged, then a decision from the desk instead of silence.
09:30 — The drill (:30) Turn to a partner and say the two sentences out loud, once each: the request for the number, and the call the next morning. Say both out loud, then swap. Ten minutes for the room, then questions.

What the room does the next morning

Training that cannot be observed is just a speech. These are the things the desk can walk the floor and see by the end of the first week.

Before the greeting

  • Every guest greeted inside 30 seconds
  • Guest name written down in front of them
  • Mobile number collected before the walkaround

During the visit

  • The right vehicle identified and logged
  • Notes written at the vehicle, not after
  • One vehicle presented well, not three badly

At the desk

  • Number presented before the trade conversation
  • Next contact agreed before the guest leaves
  • Interest level logged, not guessed from memory

After the visit

  • Same-day note in the CRM
  • 24-hour personal call or text
  • 72-hour second touch with something useful
  • Five touches over fourteen days, then thirty days to decide

How I would measure it

What gets walked on a Friday is what gets worked on Monday. The segment only counts if the store can see it in the numbers.

Logged walk-ins

Every guest, every day

Names written at hello against names with a next contact set, walked daily by the desk. The gap door and the CRM is the first number the training team fixes.

Same-day follow-up

24-hour contact rate

Percentage of logged walk-ins with a real contact inside one day — not a mass email and not a template blast.

Appointment set rate

Next contact booked

Walk-ins that leave with a day, a time and an owner. This is the number that turns a training habit into a store result.

What an unlogged walk-in costs

The first number this training fixes is not close rate. It is the gap between the people who walked through the door and the names in the CRM. Here is the arithmetic the room will see, with the floor assumptions stated up front so anybody can swap in their own store.

40Walk-ins per week — this store's traffic count
12.4%Of qualified leads never logged in the CRM (published benchmark)
5Lost names per week (40 × 12.4%)
10%Close rate on a worked lead
1 / 2 weeksDeliveries nobody could call (5 × 10%)
24 / yearThe same loss, every year

Sources — 12.4% of qualified leads were never logged in the dealer CRM: Better Car People, 2023 Dealer Benchmark Report. No published benchmark exists for walk-ins specifically, and a walk-in leaves no digital trail at all, so 12.4% is the floor, not the ceiling. The 40 walk-ins and the 10% close rate are placeholders for this store's own numbers.

Why this is the first fix

A lead that was never written down cannot be called, cannot be counted and cannot be coached. Every other number in the store — close rate, appointment rate, follow-up compliance — is measured against a denominator that is short by whatever walked out unrecorded.

What the training does about it

It removes the excuse. The guest's name and number are written in front of the guest, by the consultant, before the walkaround — not typed into the CRM at the end of the day from memory.

What the desk measures

Names written at hello against names with a next contact set, walked daily. The target is not a better percentage; it is every logged name carrying a day, a time and an owner.

These are illustrative floor assumptions, not claimed store data. The method is the point: substitute the store's real traffic count and logged opportunities and the same arithmetic returns the store's own number. That swap is the first thing the trainer does on day one.

Walk-in Standard — one page

Every guest, every visit. Taught the same way at every store, audited the same way by the desk.
Four steps on the floor
  1. Greet and log. Inside 30 seconds. Name and mobile written in front of the guest.
  2. Discover and write the right vehicle. Who is it for, what are they driving now, what must be true on delivery day.
  3. Present one vehicle well. Feature, benefit, proof — notes written at the vehicle, not after.
  4. The desk prices it, you present it. You land the guest on one unit, then the desk produces the pricing for the customer — price, trade allowance, payment, term, rate, taxes and fees — then go for the close and ask for the paperwork with the manager in the room. Cannot close? Agree on the day to follow up — a date on the calendar, not “soon”. They drive off with a day, a time and an owner. Tax, registration and doc fees follow where the vehicle is registered, so the ZIP asked for is the customer’s, not the store’s — with their full address, the pricing is exact rather than an average.
Five follow-up clocks
  • Same day — note in the CRM and a call while the visit is still fresh.
  • 24 hours — a personal call or text about their car, never a template.
  • 72 hours — a second touch with something useful: a vehicle, a payment, an answer.
  • 14 → 30 days — five touches by day fourteen, a written reason and a tail to thirty.
  • Nurture — monthly and only for the engaged buyer, running until they buy: the average buying cycle is 89 days, so the ones still looking at day thirty are early, not dead. one reason a month, timed to when they said they would move. No response and no engagement ends the file at thirty.
One number
  • Names logged against names with a next contact. That gap is the only number this training is scored on in week one.
  • Walked daily by the desk. Reviewed Friday in the same ten-minute window.
  • One win, one miss, one thing the room drills again next week.
