Inside the systems — CRM, process, inventory

At Jaguar Land Rover Newport Beach I am wired into the systems the store runs on, and everything the client hears from me comes out of them:

  • CRM and pipeline discipline. Every relationship lives in eLead CRM — first contact, follow-up cadence, task queues, notes, outcomes. I work the queue daily, so nothing goes cold and no follow-up is dropped. That is records management in practice.
  • Process and record ownership. I own the record as a transaction moves: order and allocation updates, scheduling, status changes and the internal handoffs between departments. The system stays current because I keep it current.
  • Inventory and location. I track units through Dealergard / Stargard GPS and inventory status — in transit, on the lot, at the shop — so a customer gets an answer, not a guess. Same discipline as tracking one work stream across thirty of them.
  • Reporting that gets used. Regional KPIs, acquisition and inventory metrics, and the weekly numbers management actually made decisions with. I have built that reporting from scratch more than once.
  • Why it matters here. A business process analyst is asked to understand the system of record, keep it true, and prove the change worked. That is the job I already do, in a dealership and on my own platform.
