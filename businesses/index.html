How the SBA 7(a) math works

Every deal below is under $500K, which fits the SBA 7(a) sweet spot: 90% financing, 10% down, up to 10 years. We compute the debt service and DSCR on the seller-reported cash flow so you can see the real picture before you call a broker.

1. What you put down

  • 10% down on the purchase price (SBA 7(a) standard)
  • A $300K business needs ~$30K down + closing costs
  • No collateral needed for loans under $500K — SBA 90% guarantee

2. What the loan costs

  • Rates roughly 7%–9%, fixed via SBIC lending
  • Term up to 10 years for business acquisition
  • $300K loan ≈ ~$3,100/mo debt service

3. The 1.25x rule

  • Lenders want DSCR ≥ 1.25: cash flow at least 1.25× the annual loan payment
  • Quick test: cash flow ÷ price ≥ 10.5%
  • We flag every deal's DSCR on the card — green = lender-comfortable

How the SBA Score works

Every deal is scored 0–100 across five factors. The badge on each card shows the total and grade — the breakdown line under it shows where the points came from.

Value · 30 pts

  • Asking price ÷ cash flow (the multiple)
  • < 2x = 30 · < 3x = 25 · < 4x = 20 · < 5x = 14 · < 6x = 8 · < 8x = 4
  • Lower multiple = pay less for the same cash flow

Debt Coverage · 30 pts

  • DSCR on a 7% / 10-yr SBA 7(a) loan, 10% down
  • ≥ 2.5x = 30 · ≥ 2.0x = 26 · ≥ 1.5x = 20 · ≥ 1.25x = 14 (lender bar) · ≥ 1.0x = 6
  • Heaviest weight — this is what the bank checks first

Cash Yield · 15 pts

  • Cash flow ÷ asking price, per year
  • ≥ 40% = 15 · ≥ 30% = 12 · ≥ 25% = 9 · ≥ 20% = 6 · ≥ 15% = 3
  • How fast the business pays back the purchase price

Cash Flow Size · 10 pts

  • Absolute seller-reported SDE
  • ≥ $150K = 10 · ≥ $100K = 8 · ≥ $75K = 6 · ≥ $50K = 4 · ≥ $30K = 2
  • Bigger cushion = survives a slow year + pays you

Moat & Motivation · 15 pts

  • Established years (+4) · owner retiring (+3)
  • Price reduced (+2) · real estate included (+2)
  • Franchise / absentee / turnkey (+2) · net income stated (+1)
  • Signals a real, defensible, motivated-to-sell business
