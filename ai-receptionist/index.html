What it answers. What it escalates.

The line between the two is the whole product. A receptionist that improvises a price or a completion time costs more than an unanswered call.

✅ Answers, books, confirms

  • Hours, address, directions, which drive to pull into
  • Service, parts and sales phone numbers and pickup times
  • Appointment requests with vehicle, mileage and concern
  • Reschedules and cancellations on an existing booking
  • Repair-order status when connected to the RO system
  • Loaner and courtesy-vehicle requests — requested, not promised
  • Recall questions: VIN captured, advisor confirms eligibility
  • Whether a part is orderable, once parts confirms availability
  • Warm transfer to any department, with context

↗ Escalates, never improvises

  • Repair prices, diagnosis, and "what will this cost"
  • Promising a completion date or a wait time
  • Declaring a car safe to drive on a brake or warning-light call
  • Warranty coverage decisions
  • Goodwill, discounts, or anything a manager signs off on
  • Confirming a loaner is available today
  • Quoting a trade or a payment — that's sales
  • Anything a caller asks twice and the system can't see

How a call actually runs

Same path whether it's 7:05am on a Tuesday or 11pm on a Sunday.

1. Ring once, answer, disclose

"Jaguar Land Rover Newport Beach service, this is RoyceAI — how can I help?" Disclosure is in the first sentence, and a caller can ask for a person at any point.

2. Classify the call

Book · status · safety · parts · sales · billing question · complaint. The classification sets which questions get asked and where the call ends.

3. Capture the vehicle

Year, model, mileage, VIN or plate when offered, last name, callback number. Existing customers are matched on the number so the advisor isn't re-asking.

4. Offer a real slot

Read from your live availability — Thu 7:30, Fri 1:00 — never a made-up time. Two options, one question.

5. Confirm, then route

Text confirmation to the caller, appointment or message to the advisor's queue, transcript attached. Urgent flags go to the top.

6. Leave a record

Transcript, capture fields, outcome, handle time. The Monday report is generated from those records — answer rate by hour, bookings, after-hours split.

Questions a service manager actually asks

Will it quote a price for a brake job?

No. Pricing and diagnosis stay with the advisor. It captures the vehicle, mileage and concern, books the slot, and leaves the advisor what they need to price it. A receptionist that guesses a price creates a promise the store has to honour.

What if the caller asks for a human?

It transfers immediately and says who it's transferring to. If the advisor doesn't pick up, the caller gets a callback promise and the message lands in the advisor's queue with the transcript.

Can it tell a caller their car is ready?

Only when it's connected to your repair-order system. Without that read it says the advisor will confirm, and it pages the advisor instead of guessing. That distinction is the reason advisors trust it.

Does it tell people they're talking to an AI?

Yes — in the greeting. Every call also opens with a recording notice, and the caller can opt out of the recording.

How does it get our availability?

Either from your scheduling system's API, or from a published availability file your service manager controls. If neither exists, it takes the request and books against slots your advisor confirms — slower, but never a time you can't staff.

What happens if the line drops mid-call?

The capture fields it already has are saved with the transcript, the advisor sees a dropped call with a callback number, and the caller gets a text. Nothing is lost because the call ended early.

How long until it's live?

Days, not months. Week one: number forwarding, hours, departments, and the appointment rules. Week two: your real availability feed and the advisor queue. The 14-day trial runs on the live line.
