The Home Equity Opportunity

Why this focus, in numbers: what the category is, who's in it, and why the contractor channel is still open. Sources — CFPB home-equity market research, HomeWealthIQ/Barastone public materials, and industry press (2024-2026).

🏦

$35T of trapped equity

US homeowners hold $35T+ in home equity (CFPB), yet the entire home-equity-contract market is only $2-3B — under 1% of the $186B home-equity lending market. The room above this category is the size of a decade of growth.

🚀

A category compounding

Originations hit ~$1.5B in 2024 and were projected to double or triple in 2025; rated securitizations are the engine (DBRS-rated since 2023, volume nearly doubled again in 2025, $235M single deals by 2026). Institutions are building the rails.

🏠

The homeowner fit

Typical HEA customers: 50s-60s, 30%+ equity, project cash need — solar, battery, ADU, remodel, or debt consolidation. No monthly payments for 10 years, approval by equity not credit score, recorded second-position lien, cost capped at 20% annualized. The alternative to a HELOC they don't want.

🛡️

Compliant by design

Platform Partners market and introduce; Barastone does the regulated work — applications, underwriting, funding. Cost capped at 20% annualized no matter the home's appreciation. CFPB and state regulators are watching this category — outreach copy stays inside Barastone's disclosure lane, and RoyceAI keeps that story consistent everywhere.

☀️

Solar is the perfect trigger

California solar dealers are squeezed by NEM 3.0 and financing stacks that kill otherwise-eligible homeowners — every one of them has a folder of "couldn't finance" leads. A $0-down, no-monthly-payment option reopens those files today.

💎

No one owns the contractor channel

Hometap, Unison, and Point are well-funded with institutional capital — but none recruit contractor partners at scale. The CA solar channel is open, receptive, and Neil is recruiting it first. That wedge is the whole game.
