Where RoyceAI fits

AmaTone is a voice product asking people to trust a black box. That is exactly the gap AI tooling closes — and it is a live prospect, not a hypothetical.

🎤 The core problem

A voice-biometric app has to be credible to a clinician before a corporate buyer will sign. Right now the product asserts insight from a 30-second recording; there is no visible reasoning layer behind the score. That gap is a build, not a marketing fix.

🤖 Conversational layer

An AI assistant that explains each reading in plain language — what changed in the voice, what it likely means, what to do next. Voice in, guidance out. This is what turns a passive dashboard into something a user opens daily.

📞 Voice intake, 24/7

An AI receptionist front door for the B2B motion: answers corporate wellness inquiries around the clock, qualifies the buyer, books the demo, and hands a warmed lead to a human. That is RoyceAI's flagship product today.

📈 Data into decisions

The app already collects voice samples. The missing layer is trend detection and reporting — nudge a user when a pattern shifts, generate the corporate wellness report the employer actually pays for. Passive collection becomes an active product.

⚡ Ship speed

He has run 1,500 hours of development to reach 70+ testers. A focused AI build compresses the interrogate-measure-report loop into weeks, not quarters — and he has already proven he can ship under his own name.

💰 The commercial read

Founder of a 1–10 person company, in a new retail job, with a mark he re-filed in 2026 — he is still betting on this. A small operator with a stalled consumer product is a realistic buyer for a scoped AI build, not a fantasy enterprise deal.

Positioning, if we approach

  • Lead with the receptionist, not the rebuild. He needs corporate leads handled before he needs a new app. 24/7 AI intake is a $3,000/mo product we already run — a low-friction first yes.
  • Then scope the intelligence layer. Explanation, trend detection and reporting are a defined build, quotable as a one-time project against his stated product goals.
  • Speak his language. He talks IRB, validation and white papers. Frame AI as evidence infrastructure — traceable, reviewable, defensible to a clinic — not as a gadget.
  • Respect the founder's seat. He runs his own company and will want authorship. Pitch a partner build, not a takeover of his product.
  • Do not lead with the car. Any approach framed as dealer-vs-dealer poisons the product conversation. Keep the two lanes separate.
